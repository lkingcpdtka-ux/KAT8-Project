# Results

## Adipose-specific KAT8 knockdown elicits a stronger transcriptomic response in iWAT than gWAT

Bulk RNA-seq was performed on inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT) and gonadal white adipose tissue (gWAT) from KAT8 knockdown (KAT8KD) and control (CTL) mice. After quality filtering and outlier removal (JS_08, JS_28), differential expression analysis using DESeq2 identified 3,022 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in iWAT (1,867 up-regulated and 1,155 down-regulated; FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1), compared to 1,159 DEGs in gWAT (737 up-regulated and 422 down-regulated; FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1). The nearly three-fold difference in DEG count indicates that iWAT is substantially more sensitive to KAT8 loss than gWAT. This depot-specific vulnerability likely reflects the distinct epigenomic landscapes and developmental origins of subcutaneous versus visceral adipose tissue; as a histone H4K16 acetyltransferase, KAT8 may maintain a larger fraction of the active chromatin state in iWAT, such that its loss disproportionately disrupts gene expression in this depot. Notably, both depots showed an asymmetry favoring up-regulation (62% of iWAT DEGs and 64% of gWAT DEGs), suggesting that KAT8 loss not only silences its direct acetylation targets but also triggers secondary transcriptional programs -- likely inflammatory -- that produce widespread gene induction.

## Volcano plot analysis reveals depot-specific patterns of differential expression

Volcano plots for both depots displayed the expected symmetrical distribution of fold changes, with dashed threshold lines demarcating significance (FDR < 0.05) and biological effect size (|log2FC| > 1) cutoffs (Figure X). In iWAT, the most statistically significant genes included *Hint2* (log2FC = -1.26, padj = 8.19 x 10^-73), *Gm44386* (log2FC = -4.70, padj = 4.85 x 10^-70), and *Pxmp2* (log2FC = -2.57, padj = 1.29 x 10^-63), all down-regulated. The prominence of *Hint2*, a mitochondrial nucleotide hydrolase involved in oxidative phosphorylation, and *Pxmp2*, a peroxisomal membrane channel protein, among the top hits foreshadows the global loss of mitochondrial and peroxisomal function that emerged from pathway analyses. Among the genes of interest highlighted on the EnhancedVolcano plots, *Mmp12* was the most prominently up-regulated gene (log2FC = 4.40 in iWAT; log2FC = 3.36 in gWAT), while *Timp4* was the most prominently down-regulated (log2FC = -2.67 in iWAT; log2FC = -2.11 in gWAT). The reciprocal regulation of *Mmp12* (a macrophage-derived elastase) and *Timp4* (its endogenous inhibitor) in both depots points to a conserved shift in the protease-antiprotease balance favoring ECM degradation and tissue remodeling upon KAT8 loss. In iWAT, *Mmp16* (log2FC = 1.91, padj = 1.02 x 10^-3) and *Mmp14* (log2FC = 0.67, padj = 1.89 x 10^-9) were also significantly up-regulated, whereas *Mmp3* did not reach significance (padj = 0.062). In gWAT, *Mmp3* was significant (log2FC = 1.26, padj = 0.038) along with *Mmp12* and *Mmp14* (log2FC = 0.33, padj = 7.85 x 10^-3), while *Mmp16* was not differentially expressed. The depot-specific repertoire of up-regulated MMPs suggests that while both tissues engage matrix remodeling machinery in response to KAT8 loss, the specific proteases deployed differ -- potentially reflecting differences in the resident immune cell populations or the structural composition of the ECM between subcutaneous and visceral fat.

## Genes of interest heatmap reveals coordinated suppression of adipogenic and metabolic programs in iWAT

A focused heatmap of 29 pre-selected genes of interest -- encompassing collagens/ECM components, adipocyte markers and metabolic regulators, and matrix remodeling enzymes -- was generated using variance-stabilized expression values (Z-scored relative to control means) and visualized with ComplexHeatmap. In iWAT, the heatmap displayed a clear separation between CTL and KAT8KD groups, with two dominant expression patterns. The first cluster comprised adipocyte identity and lipid metabolism genes that were uniformly down-regulated in KAT8KD samples: *Pparg* (log2FC = -1.54, padj = 5.66 x 10^-20), *Ppara* (log2FC = -2.15, padj = 2.38 x 10^-16), *Plin1* (log2FC = -1.61, padj = 5.57 x 10^-15), *Srebf1* (log2FC = -1.22, padj = 3.37 x 10^-15), *Fabp4* (log2FC = -1.02, padj = 1.17 x 10^-10), *Sorbs1* (log2FC = -1.07, padj = 2.18 x 10^-8), *Ppargc1a* (log2FC = -1.71, padj = 1.03 x 10^-8), *Lep* (log2FC = -1.04, padj = 6.22 x 10^-5), *Fabp5* (log2FC = -1.10, padj = 3.47 x 10^-6), and *Cd36* (log2FC = -0.93, padj = 2.46 x 10^-6). *Cav1* (log2FC = -0.77, padj = 4.90 x 10^-7) and *Angptl4* (log2FC = -0.59, padj = 1.72 x 10^-3) were also reduced, though with fold changes below the |log2FC| > 1 threshold. The simultaneous down-regulation of both master transcriptional regulators of adipogenesis (*Pparg*, *Ppara*, *Srebf1*) and their downstream functional targets (*Fabp4*, *Plin1*, *Cd36*, *Lep*) suggests that KAT8 is required for maintaining the mature adipocyte transcriptional program in iWAT. The loss of *Ppargc1a*, a key regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and thermogenesis, is particularly notable as it connects the adipocyte identity defect to the mitochondrial dysfunction observed in pathway analyses. Reduced *Lep* expression further implies impaired endocrine function of iWAT adipocytes following KAT8 knockdown.

The second cluster consisted of collagen and ECM genes that were up-regulated in iWAT KAT8KD samples. *Col6a5* showed the largest fold change (log2FC = 2.41, padj = 1.13 x 10^-3), followed by *Col6a6* (log2FC = 1.45, padj = 2.56 x 10^-7), *Col4a5* (log2FC = 1.17, padj = 1.34 x 10^-6), *Col13a1* (log2FC = 1.08, padj = 3.99 x 10^-5), *Col14a1* (log2FC = 0.99, padj = 2.73 x 10^-5), *Col16a1* (log2FC = 0.91, padj = 7.54 x 10^-6), and *Col27a1* (log2FC = 0.78, padj = 3.21 x 10^-5). By contrast, basement membrane collagens *Col4a1* (log2FC = -0.48, padj = 1.92 x 10^-3), *Col4a2* (log2FC = -0.56, padj = 5.28 x 10^-5), *Col15a1* (log2FC = -0.45, padj = 6.15 x 10^-3), and *Col5a3* (log2FC = -0.51, padj = 6.37 x 10^-3) were modestly down-regulated. *Fn1* was up-regulated (log2FC = 0.83, padj = 8.76 x 10^-9). The selective up-regulation of fibrillar and pericellular collagens (Col6a5, Col6a6, Col13a1, Col14a1) alongside the down-regulation of basement membrane components (Col4a1, Col4a2) is consistent with adipose tissue fibrosis -- a hallmark of metabolically dysfunctional adipose tissue. Collagen VI in particular is a well-established marker of adipose fibrosis that constrains adipocyte expansion and promotes metabolic dysfunction. The concurrent induction of *Fn1* (fibronectin) reinforces a profibrotic ECM remodeling program triggered by KAT8 loss. Together with the up-regulation of MMPs and down-regulation of *Timp4*, these data suggest active restructuring of the iWAT extracellular matrix from a basement membrane-rich adipocyte-supportive architecture to a fibrotic, collagen-dense stroma.

In gWAT, the same gene order was applied to enable direct comparison. The adipocyte marker and metabolic gene cluster showed no significant changes (e.g., *Ppara* log2FC = 0.006, padj = 0.99; *Plin1* log2FC = -0.39, padj = 0.11; *Fabp4* log2FC = -0.03, padj = 0.90), with the exception of *Pparg* (log2FC = -0.39, padj = 0.039) and *Fabp5* (log2FC = 0.71, padj = 5.46 x 10^-3; notably up-regulated in gWAT, in contrast to its down-regulation in iWAT). Collagen genes were uniformly non-significant in gWAT, with only *Col27a1* reaching significance (log2FC = 0.64, padj = 2.18 x 10^-3). Among the matrix remodeling genes, *Mmp12* (log2FC = 3.36, padj = 3.14 x 10^-9) and *Timp4* (log2FC = -2.11, padj = 2.36 x 10^-12) showed large and significant changes in both depots, while *Mmp3* (log2FC = 1.26, padj = 0.038) and *Mmp14* (log2FC = 0.33, padj = 7.85 x 10^-3) were significant only in gWAT at more modest fold changes. The near-complete preservation of the adipocyte transcriptional program in gWAT -- despite robust immune and MMP activation -- indicates that the metabolic consequences of KAT8 loss are depot-selective. The shared dysregulation of the Mmp12/Timp4 axis across both depots, however, suggests a common inflammatory remodeling response that is decoupled from the adipocyte identity loss seen only in iWAT. The opposing direction of *Fabp5* between depots (down in iWAT, up in gWAT) may reflect depot-specific roles for this lipid chaperone; in gWAT, *Fabp5* induction could represent a compensatory response to macrophage infiltration, as FABP5 is highly expressed in activated macrophages.

## ORA of KEGG pathways identifies metabolic suppression and immune activation in iWAT

Over-representation analysis (ORA) of KEGG pathways was performed separately for up-regulated and down-regulated DEGs in each depot.

Among the 1,155 down-regulated DEGs in iWAT, 41 KEGG pathways were significantly enriched (adjusted p < 0.05). The most enriched pathway was PPAR signaling pathway (23 genes, adjusted p = 9.42 x 10^-11), which included core adipogenic regulators *Pparg*, *Ppara*, *Plin1*, *Fabp4*, *Adipoq*, *Scd1*, *Pck1*, and *Ucp1*. Additional metabolic pathways enriched among down-regulated genes included carbon metabolism (24 genes, adjusted p = 3.46 x 10^-7), valine/leucine/isoleucine degradation (16 genes, adjusted p = 4.03 x 10^-7), fatty acid metabolism (14 genes, adjusted p = 5.10 x 10^-5), fatty acid biosynthesis (7 genes, adjusted p = 7.03 x 10^-5), propanoate metabolism (13 genes, adjusted p = 4.03 x 10^-7), the citrate cycle/TCA cycle (7 genes, adjusted p = 9.80 x 10^-3), glycolysis/gluconeogenesis (10 genes, adjusted p = 0.010), and pyruvate metabolism (13 genes, adjusted p = 8.37 x 10^-6). The AMPK signaling pathway (24 genes, adjusted p = 1.41 x 10^-6), insulin signaling pathway (21 genes, adjusted p = 1.76 x 10^-4), adipocytokine signaling pathway (11 genes, adjusted p = 9.80 x 10^-3), glucagon signaling pathway (18 genes, adjusted p = 9.44 x 10^-5), and regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes (13 genes, adjusted p = 9.44 x 10^-5) were also enriched. Peroxisome (15 genes, adjusted p = 5.16 x 10^-4), glutathione metabolism (12 genes, adjusted p = 8.83 x 10^-4), and xenobiotic metabolism by cytochrome P450 (14 genes, adjusted p = 1.08 x 10^-6) pathways were enriched among the down-regulated genes as well. Collectively, the down-regulated KEGG landscape in iWAT reveals a near-complete collapse of adipocyte metabolic capacity: the PPAR signaling axis that drives adipocyte differentiation and lipid storage, the AMPK and insulin signaling networks that mediate metabolic sensing, and the downstream effector pathways for fatty acid synthesis, oxidation, and glucose utilization are all coordinately suppressed. The enrichment of peroxisomal and detoxification pathways (glutathione metabolism, cytochrome P450 xenobiotic metabolism) further suggests that the metabolic impairment extends beyond core lipid handling to auxiliary metabolic functions. The presence of *Ucp1* in the PPAR signaling gene list is noteworthy, as it indicates that the thermogenic potential of iWAT is also compromised by KAT8 loss.

Among the 1,867 up-regulated DEGs in iWAT, 21 KEGG pathways were significantly enriched. ECM-receptor interaction (26 genes, adjusted p = 8.47 x 10^-8) was the most significantly enriched pathway, followed by neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (36 genes, adjusted p = 3.89 x 10^-6), protein digestion and absorption (21 genes, adjusted p = 2.50 x 10^-5), cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (31 genes, adjusted p = 0.013), and cell adhesion molecule interaction (24 genes, adjusted p = 0.019). PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (44 genes, adjusted p = 0.020), calcium signaling pathway (31 genes, adjusted p = 0.033), and cAMP signaling pathway (28 genes, adjusted p = 0.033) were also enriched. The up-regulated pathway profile in iWAT reveals a tissue undergoing active structural remodeling and immune infiltration. The dominance of ECM-receptor interaction as the top enriched pathway -- containing integrins, laminins, and collagens -- is consistent with fibrotic remodeling and altered cell-matrix interactions. The co-enrichment of cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and cell adhesion molecules reflects immune cell recruitment and activation. The enrichment of PI3K-Akt signaling among up-regulated genes likely reflects proliferative signaling in non-adipocyte stromal and immune cells, rather than canonical insulin-stimulated PI3K-Akt activity in adipocytes.

In gWAT, ORA of the 737 up-regulated DEGs identified 9 significant KEGG pathways, all immune-related. Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (29 genes, adjusted p = 1.94 x 10^-7) and viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptor (17 genes, adjusted p = 2.17 x 10^-7) were the most significant, followed by IL-17 signaling pathway (12 genes, adjusted p = 3.70 x 10^-3), neutrophil extracellular trap formation (15 genes, adjusted p = 0.011), chemokine signaling pathway (18 genes, adjusted p = 0.013), tuberculosis (16 genes, adjusted p = 0.024), and C-type lectin receptor signaling pathway (12 genes, adjusted p = 0.045). No KEGG pathways were significantly enriched among the 422 down-regulated gWAT DEGs. The gWAT ORA results present a striking contrast to iWAT: the response is almost purely inflammatory, with no metabolic pathways disrupted. The enrichment of IL-17 signaling, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, and C-type lectin receptor signaling points to activation of both innate and adaptive immune arms, with particular involvement of myeloid cell populations. The tuberculosis and viral protein interaction pathways, while nominally pathogen-related, represent pattern recognition and cytokine signaling modules that are broadly activated in sterile inflammation -- their enrichment here likely reflects macrophage activation and interferon-related signaling rather than infectious processes. The complete absence of enriched pathways among down-regulated gWAT genes reinforces the conclusion that KAT8 knockdown does not impair the metabolic program of visceral adipose tissue.

## fGSEA of KEGG pathways confirms metabolic program suppression in iWAT and immune activation in gWAT

Functional gene set enrichment analysis (fGSEA) using the DESeq2 Wald statistic as a ranking metric identified 165 significant KEGG pathways in iWAT (padj < 0.05). The pathways with the most negative normalized enrichment scores (NES) -- indicating coordinate down-regulation -- were oxidative phosphorylation (NES = -3.25), thermogenesis (NES = -3.12), valine/leucine/isoleucine degradation (NES = -2.93), ribosome (NES = -2.82), peroxisome (NES = -2.82), propanoate metabolism (NES = -2.81), carbon metabolism (NES = -2.80), non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NES = -2.80), citrate cycle (NES = -2.78), and diabetic cardiomyopathy (NES = -2.77). Among the positively enriched pathways, osteoclast differentiation (NES = 2.61), cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (NES = 2.39), ECM-receptor interaction (NES = 2.39), and regulation of actin cytoskeleton (NES = 2.18) were the most prominent. The fGSEA results provide a complementary and more nuanced view than ORA, as they leverage the full ranked gene list rather than a binary significant/non-significant cutoff. The emergence of oxidative phosphorylation as the single most strongly down-regulated pathway (NES = -3.25) reveals that mitochondrial electron transport chain dysfunction is the most coordinated transcriptional change in KAT8-deficient iWAT. Together with thermogenesis (NES = -3.12), these results suggest a profound impairment of mitochondrial energy production capacity. The negative enrichment of ribosome (NES = -2.82) was not detected in the ORA analysis and suggests a broader translational suppression accompanying the metabolic collapse. The enrichment of disease-associated pathways (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, diabetic cardiomyopathy) reflects the shared metabolic gene modules between these conditions and the KAT8 KD phenotype, consistent with KAT8 loss creating a metabolically dysfunctional adipose tissue state. On the positive side, the enrichment of osteoclast differentiation (NES = 2.61) likely reflects activation of NF-kB and MAPK signaling modules shared between osteoclastogenesis and macrophage inflammatory programs, rather than literal bone resorption in adipose tissue.

In gWAT, 119 significant KEGG pathways were identified, with a markedly different enrichment profile. Only 5 pathways had negative NES values, including mannose type O-glycan biosynthesis (NES = -1.95), ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling (NES = -1.48), and lysine degradation (NES = -1.62). The vast majority (114 pathways) had positive NES values. The most positively enriched pathways included phagosome (NES = 2.53), tuberculosis (NES = 2.53), osteoclast differentiation (NES = 2.44), natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity (NES = 2.41), Epstein-Barr virus infection (NES = 2.37), cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (NES = 2.28), and apoptosis (NES = 2.23). The gWAT fGSEA profile is dominated by a nearly uniform upward shift in immune and inflammatory gene sets, with 114 of 119 significant pathways positively enriched. The predominance of phagosome, natural killer cell cytotoxicity, and apoptosis pathways suggests active immune surveillance and cell turnover in gWAT. The negative enrichment of ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling (NES = -1.48) is intriguing in the context of KAT8's role as a chromatin modifier -- this may reflect a compensatory or cascading disruption of other chromatin remodeling complexes following loss of H4K16 acetylation. Critically, the absence of oxidative phosphorylation, thermogenesis, PPAR signaling, and other metabolic pathways from the negatively enriched set in gWAT confirms that the metabolic collapse observed in iWAT is not a general consequence of KAT8 loss in adipose tissue, but rather a depot-specific phenomenon.

## fGSEA of GO Biological Process terms corroborates depot-specific functional impacts (Supplementary)

fGSEA using GO Biological Process (GO:BP) terms with semantic similarity-based simplification (cutoff = 0.5) identified 43 significant terms in iWAT and 33 in gWAT (padj < 0.05).

In iWAT, the 13 terms with negative NES encompassed mitochondrial bioenergetics and catabolic processes: proton motive force-driven mitochondrial ATP synthesis (NES = -3.20), mitochondrial translation (NES = -3.17), mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I assembly (NES = -3.09), fatty acid beta-oxidation (NES = -2.93), mitochondrial electron transport NADH to ubiquinone (NES = -2.78), protein transmembrane import into intracellular organelle (NES = -2.58), ATP metabolic process (NES = -2.43), mitochondrial transmembrane transport (NES = -2.42), and lipid catabolic process (NES = -2.37). The 30 positively enriched terms reflected immune and structural remodeling processes: leukocyte degranulation (NES = 2.77), antigen receptor-mediated signaling pathway (NES = 2.38), positive regulation of leukocyte migration (NES = 2.33), positive regulation of cytokine production (NES = 2.28), regulation of actin cytoskeleton organization (NES = 2.08), and leukocyte activation involved in immune response (NES = 2.07). The GO:BP analysis provides the most mechanistically specific view of the mitochondrial impairment in iWAT. The fact that the three most strongly down-regulated biological processes are proton motive force-driven ATP synthesis, mitochondrial translation, and respiratory chain complex I assembly -- each representing a distinct aspect of mitochondrial function -- indicates that KAT8 loss does not merely suppress one branch of mitochondrial activity but rather causes a comprehensive breakdown of mitochondrial biogenesis and function. The down-regulation of mitochondrial translation and protein import further suggests that KAT8 loss may impair the nuclear-encoded mitochondrial transcriptional program at its most fundamental level, potentially through loss of H4K16ac at the promoters of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes. On the immune side, the enrichment of leukocyte degranulation as the top positive GO:BP term (NES = 2.77), along with antigen receptor signaling and cytokine production, indicates that the immune infiltrate in iWAT is not merely present but actively engaged in effector functions.

In gWAT, only 3 terms had negative NES -- intraciliary transport (NES = -2.42), tRNA metabolic process (NES = -2.12), and cilium assembly (NES = -1.96) -- none of which relate to adipocyte metabolism. The 30 positively enriched terms were dominated by immune activation: antigen processing and presentation of exogenous peptide antigen (NES = 2.69), leukocyte degranulation (NES = 2.66), myeloid cell activation involved in immune response (NES = 2.62), positive regulation of tumor necrosis factor production (NES = 2.48), production of molecular mediator involved in inflammatory response (NES = 2.37), leukocyte apoptotic process (NES = 2.31), regulation of lymphocyte proliferation (NES = 2.29), and positive regulation of leukocyte migration (NES = 2.26). The gWAT GO:BP results underscore the purely inflammatory nature of the gWAT response to KAT8 loss. The negative enrichment of cilium assembly and intraciliary transport may reflect suppression of primary cilia signaling in adipocyte progenitors, which has been implicated in adipocyte differentiation. The immune terms enriched in gWAT are notable for their specificity: antigen processing and presentation of exogenous peptide antigen (NES = 2.69) suggests activation of professional antigen-presenting cells (macrophages/dendritic cells), while positive regulation of TNF production (NES = 2.48) and production of molecular mediator involved in inflammatory response (NES = 2.37) indicate a classically activated, pro-inflammatory immune milieu. The enrichment of both lymphocyte proliferation and myeloid cell activation indicates engagement of both innate and adaptive immune arms in gWAT, consistent with a chronic inflammatory state rather than an acute immune response.
