# Revised Results Draft (Minor Edits + Parenthetical Check Notes)

## Results

### Adipocyte-specific loss of KAT8 produces depot-dependent transcriptional dysregulation in white adipose tissue

To characterize the transcriptomic consequences of KAT8 loss in adipocytes, we performed bulk RNA-seq on inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT) and gonadal white adipose tissue (gWAT) from adipocyte-specific KAT8 knockout (AKO) and floxed control (FL) mice (n = 5 per group per depot per sex). Male and female samples were combined within each depot for the primary differential expression analysis, as formal Sex × Genotype interaction testing (DESeq2 likelihood ratio test) indicated that most AKO transcriptional effects were sex-independent within each depot (check Supplemental Fig. X / interaction table), and sex-stratified fold changes were highly concordant (check Supplemental Fig. Y).

Principal component analysis of variance-stabilized counts revealed separation by adipose depot and genotype across PC1 and PC2 (check exact PC1/PC2 variance percentages in final PCA panel; currently listed as 27% and 14.7%). KAT8 AKO samples segregated from FL controls in both depots, with a stronger genotype effect in iWAT. Hierarchical clustering of top DEGs (|log2FC| > 1, FDR < 0.05) showed robust FL vs AKO separation in both depots (check Supplemental Fig. B legend thresholds if changed).

KEGG over-representation analysis (ORA) revealed depot-specific functional consequences of KAT8 loss. Among downregulated genes in iWAT, significantly enriched pathways included PPAR signaling (p.adj = 8.3 × 10^-11), carbon metabolism (p.adj = 3.1 × 10^-7), propanoate metabolism (p.adj = 3.7 × 10^-7), valine/leucine/isoleucine degradation (p.adj = 3.7 × 10^-7), metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450 (p.adj = 1.0 × 10^-6), AMPK signaling (p.adj = 1.3 × 10^-6), pyruvate metabolism (p.adj = 7.8 × 10^-6), fatty acid metabolism (p.adj = 4.8 × 10^-5), regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes (p.adj = 8.7 × 10^-5), and insulin signaling (p.adj = 1.6 × 10^-4). Upregulated genes in iWAT were most significantly enriched in ECM-receptor interaction (p.adj = 8.3 × 10^-8) and cornified envelope formation (p.adj = 8.5 × 10^-8), followed by neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (p.adj = 3.8 × 10^-6), plus cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and PI3K-Akt signaling.

In gWAT, upregulated genes were enriched in cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (p.adj = 2.0 × 10^-7), viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptor (p.adj = 2.2 × 10^-7), IL-17 signaling (p.adj = 3.7 × 10^-3), neutrophil extracellular trap formation (p.adj = 0.011), and chemokine signaling (p.adj = 0.013), reflecting a pronounced inflammatory transcriptional signature. No KEGG pathways reached significance among gWAT downregulated genes by ORA.

Volcano plot analysis showed a broader dynamic range of fold changes among DEGs in iWAT (check final plotted DEG label list if auto-label settings were updated). In iWAT, downregulated genes included lipid/intermediary metabolism genes (Pxmp2, Ivd, Eci3, Acsm5, Cidea, Acacb, Ppara, Pck1, Acaca, Acox1, Ucp1), glutathione/xenobiotic genes (Gstk1, Mgst3, Gsta4, Mgst2), and related metabolic genes (Amy1, Vnn3, Ces1f). Upregulated iWAT genes included inflammatory mediators (Il1rn, Gdf15, Eda2r), ECM components (Cd44, Sdc1), ion channel/calcium signaling genes (Slc8a1, Ryr2, Atp1a4, Cacna1d), and integrin family members (Itgb4, Itga2, Itgb8). In contrast, gWAT showed stronger upregulation of immune/inflammatory genes, including chemokines (Ccl8, Ccl2, Cxcl2, Cxcl10, Cxcl1, Ccl3), cytokines (Il1b, Il12b, Camp), immune receptors (Eda2r, Ctss, Il1rn, Itgb2, Itgax, Clec4d), and signaling mediators (Traf4, Fosl1, Gdf15, Atp6v0d2) (check against final volcano source table).

Examination of curated functional gene sets further illustrated depot-dependent changes. Mitochondrial and energy metabolism genes, including ETC components (Atp5pb, Ndufab1, Ndufb10, Atp5f1d, Sdhd, Ndufb8, Atp5me), mitochondrial-encoded genes (mt-Nd2, mt-Nd5), mitochondrial ribosomal proteins (Mrps10, Mrpl15, Mrpl17), and additional mitochondrial factors (Stoml2, Nsun4, Uqcc2, Ptcd1, Mtg2, Dap3, Hars1, Gars1), were strongly downregulated in iWAT AKO. A similar trend may be present for selected mitochondrial modules in gWAT, but this is less dominant than the inflammatory signature (check final gWAT fgsea directionality summary). Immune/inflammatory genes (Nckap1l, Myo1f, Cd84, Lat2, Fcer1g, Hmox1, Cd300a, Itgb2, Syk, Spi1, Tyrobp, Pycard) were upregulated in both depots. Lipid metabolism genes (Soat1, Lipa, Scarb2, Nus1, Acsl3, P2rx7, Hilpda, Pltp, Abcg1) and ECM/remodeling genes (Mdm2, Ctss, Adam8, Tcirg1, Gpnmb, Ctsk, Timp1, Lgals3, Cd44, Tnfaip8l2) were also differentially expressed.

### Conclusion

Collectively, these data show that adipocyte-specific loss of KAT8 drives broad transcriptional remodeling in both subcutaneous and visceral white adipose depots. The combination of reduced metabolic/mitochondrial programs (especially in iWAT) and increased immune-inflammatory/ECM remodeling programs indicates that KAT8 supports normal adipocyte transcriptional identity. The depot-dependent pattern—stronger metabolic/thermogenic suppression in iWAT and stronger inflammatory/chemokine induction in gWAT—is consistent with known functional differences between subcutaneous and visceral adipose tissue.
